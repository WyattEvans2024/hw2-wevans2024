RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. It combines a search over relevant source material with a language model that uses that retrieved context to answer a question.

Chunking breaks long documents into manageable pieces before indexing. Smaller chunks make retrieval faster and help the system focus on a narrow slice of relevant information.

Chunk overlap keeps nearby context at the boundaries between chunks. This helps preserve continuity and reduces the chance of losing meaning when a concept spans two adjacent sections.

Source attribution means identifying where the answer came from. Good RAG systems cite the source text or document metadata so the user can verify the evidence behind the response.
